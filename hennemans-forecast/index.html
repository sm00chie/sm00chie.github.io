How the ratings work

West swell and northeast offshore wind are favored. Swell height and period set the potential; strong onshore wind and insufficient water over the reef reduce the whole score.

Daily score: 45% best hour + 55% median of remaining daylight hours, including low-tide hours. Today covers the daylight still ahead. A good daily score starts at 7/10; fair at 5; marginal at 3. Best windows require at least 5/10 and a known tide above the low-water cutoff.

The 1.5–3 ft tide transition is a starting assumption awaiting local calibration. There is no upper-tide penalty until we establish when high water swamps this break.

Spot references: Surf-Forecast (west swell, northeast wind) and SurfSpots (mid-to-high tide).

Wave heights are Open-Meteo open-water model output, not estimated breaking wave heights at Hennemans. Tides are NOAA CO-OPS predictions for La Jolla (9410230), datum MLLW. Rating is experimental. Tide limits are provisional: at or below 1.5 ft MLLW is “Too low”; 1.5–3 ft progressively reduces the whole score; 3 ft and above gets full tide credit. Missing tide data means no rating. A best window represents one forecast hour.

Sources: open-meteo.com (marine + wind), NOAA CO-OPS (tides).
